Robot vacuum

BLDC motor, ESP32, infrared sensors, servos, custom 555-based buck converters, LTspice, KiCad

An autonomous vacuum that maps a room with infrared sensors and builds a confidence map of where cleaning is needed. A brushless DC motor drives the suction, and servos handle precise positioning.

Design process

  • Sketched the system on paper as separate modules first, to list what each part needs, then designed each module on its own and combined them into one design.
  • Simulated the full design in LTspice to check that the fundamentals work and to read the voltage and current at each point.
  • Used the simulation results to size the PCB traces.
  • Calculated voltage divider values for the multiplexer, heat loss in the voltage regulators, and PWM settings for the 555 timers.

First board revision

The first design was a single large, mostly through-hole PCB. Testing found a fault in the second buck converter, errors in the multiplexer signals and errors in the NAND gate logic.

Second revision (in progress)

  • SMD parts, which are easier to solder and scale better for production.
  • Smaller board, which lowers cost.
  • Four layers with a full ground plane, giving a short ground path and less EMI.
  • Digital logic and small-signal lines routed away from the large power traces to reduce noise.
  • Wide power traces on the outer layers for better heat dissipation.

Audi A3 diagnostics

2014 Audi A3. Scan tool live data, multimeter, component testing

Fault finding on my own car, working from data and measurements to the root cause before replacing any part.

  • Turbo actuator. Logged live scan tool data while driving, interpreted the readings to find the fault, then replaced and calibrated the actuator.
  • Front sensor. The sensor stopped working. Tests separated the sensor from the wiring loom, and the fault turned out to be the connector, the least expected cause. Multimeter continuity checks and voltage readings located it, and the connector was taken apart and repaired.
  • Daytime running light. Opened the headlight and inspected the modules. The rear main module was water damaged, so it was replaced and the headlight was resealed with silicone to stop the leak.
  • Component testing. Removed parts such as the diverter valve, turbo actuator and camshaft actuator and actuated each one individually to check its operation.

Interactive chessboard
